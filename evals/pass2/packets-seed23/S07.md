## Response A

### REPLY
Goal beyond the goal: a post that gets existing users to try the feature and new readers to sign up, not just a published page.

```
Write a blog post announcing [feature name] for [product]. Before drafting, read the feature's spec, PR descriptions, changelog entry and any support tickets or user requests that led to it. Find the one real problem it solves and the user who has it.

Make at least 5 openings and 5 headlines, each from a different angle (the problem, a customer story, a before/after, a surprising number, a how-to). Pick the best of each against this criterion, written down before you start: true, specific to our users, and makes a stranger want to keep reading. Have a fresh reviewer with no context pick between your top two and report which won and why.

Use real screenshots or a short screen recording of the feature, not mockups. Check every claim (speed, numbers, comparisons) against its source in the repo, docs or data; cut or flag any you can't trace. Write in our blog's voice; read three recent posts first to match it.

Deliver the post as a finished page in our blog's format (title, meta description, slug, alt text, a CTA to try the feature), plus a 3-line version for the changelog and one social post.

Judge: every claim traced to a source, a fresh reader picks this headline over the alternatives, the page reads well on a phone, and the CTA links work. A pass is all four.
```

Raised: variants for openings and headlines with a fresh-context judge, claim checking, and finishing in the blog's format. Skipped: video and anything paid. Left placeholders for the feature and product name since the original gives neither.

### REPLY
Goal beyond the goal: a post that makes the right readers try the feature, not just one that announces it.

```

- Show the feature in use with a concrete, realistic example. Mark where a screenshot or GIF goes and describe exactly what it shows.

- Score the draft 1-5 against: true, specific, makes the target reader click the CTA. Revise until every score is 4+, or two revisions give no gain.

A pass looks like: every claim sourced, the hook names the reader's problem, one CTA, and the scores at 4+ with a one-line reason each.
```
